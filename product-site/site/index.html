How it works

Nobody has to remember to check in.

  1. 1

    People sign in to Windows as usual

    A lightweight agent on each workstation reports sign-in, screen lock, unlock and sign-out to the server. Once IT has installed it, there is nothing for people to open or click.

  2. 2

    Events become a working day

    The first sign-in or unlock of the day counts as arrival. Locks, returns and departures fill in the rest, alongside any status people have set, such as work from home or leave.

  3. 3

    Everyone sees what they need

    Individuals see their own day, managers see the team that reports to them in your directory, and leadership sees organization-wide figures.

Features

Built for the whole chain of accountability.

Automatic check-in

Status is recorded from real workstation activity: checked in, away while locked, checked out. There’s no app to open and no button to forget.

Statuses and calendar

People mark work from home, TDY, scheduled leave or a late arrival on a calendar, so the people they report to know where they are.

Absence alerts

If someone hasn’t arrived by their expected time (their preferred time or their usual arrival) plus a grace period, their managers are notified and can record what happened.

A home page for everyone

Your status, the last 30 days and what’s coming up. Managers also get who’s accounted for, who’s not in yet, and the alerts waiting for their response.

Metrics for leadership

Accounted-for rate, status mix and workstation agent coverage across the organization, plus status breakdowns by organization, employee type, location and branch.

Your directory, your structure

Groups, the org chart and reporting lines come straight from LDAP. People are found through user search, and nothing about your org has to be entered twice.

Security and privacy

Designed for environments that take identity seriously.

  • Certificate sign-in. Mutual TLS with X.509 client certificates, with directory username and password as a fallback.
  • Role-based access. User, manager and administrator roles, plus per-group permissions for who can see whom.
  • Counts, not names, in organization metrics. Organization-wide figures are aggregates and never list individuals.
  • Your data stays yours. The directory remains the source of truth, and attendance records live in your own database.
